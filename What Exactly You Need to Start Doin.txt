Step 2: Begin Ground-Station Hardware Bring-UpSet up a clean project in STM32CubeIDE for your STM32F411 Blackpill19.Enable USB Device middleware configured as a Communication Device Class (Virtual Port Com / CDC). Write a simple loop to echo received PC characters back to verify your USB link works.Configure SPI1 to communicate with your local nRF24L01 module12.
Step 3: Tackle the ESP32 Controller SPI StubObtain the Controller Specification and the provided codebase for the ESP32-S3 shared controller20.Your first integration milestone is completing the stubbed nRF24 SPI driver on the ESP32 side so it can initialize its radio and enter RX mode3.
Step 4: Design Your Application Comms ProtocolDefine how you will pack your 32-byte nRF24 payload3.You need two structures:Downlink (Dongle $\rightarrow$ Gantry ESP32): Carrying state commands (Idle, Move, E-STOP, Magnet Engage) and target motion-profile coefficients421.Uplink (Gantry ESP32 $\rightarrow$ Dongle): Carrying telemetry (position, speed, load-cell readings, VFD current, and safety error codes)622.
Step 5: Initiate Anti-Sway and Path-Planning ModelingBegin mathematical modeling of the load hoisting dynamics4. Since the payload is suspended by cables (MCTR II Spooling) and moved horizontally (MCTR I Lateral Movement), it behaves like a pendulum2.In Python or MATLAB, simulate this pendulum system to model how horizontal acceleration profiles induce sway. Derive a feedforward velocity-shaping filter (such as input shaping or s-curve ramp profiles) that cancels out these sway frequencies
